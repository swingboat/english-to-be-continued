The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
Every section is a chapter with its own flat colour, floor to ceiling. The page reads like a set of posters hung in a row, held together by one typeface and one grid. The colours are not decoration: they come from the subject (the brand's packaging, the artist's record sleeves, the product's own colour ways, the categories of the content) and each chapter's colour means that chapter. Decide the sequence like a designer orders a book: a strong opener, a quiet second, a surprise in the middle, a dark or very light closer.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
Sections are full-bleed fields, each at least 90svh tall, butted directly against each other with no gap and no divider. Inside every field the same 12-column grid and the same margins apply, and each field holds one thing: a huge heading (8–12vw) with a small paragraph, or one photograph with a caption, or one list. Text colour is chosen per field for strong contrast (ink or paper, never grey). Headings start on the same left edge in every chapter, so the eye feels the system as colours change. The navigation and the footer take the colours of the first and last chapter.

## 3. Type
Kept from the brand. One typeface in two weights. Headings very large and tight; body 18–20px. Because colour is loud, the type stays plain: no italics, no second face.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. Four to six flat fields used at full strength, one per section, each with its assigned text colour. No tints, no gradients between them: the hard edge where two fields meet is the look.

## 5. Imagery
**The project has no photographs of its own yet.** First choice: **real pictures of the subject's world from the open collections**: its city and street in old postcards and photographs, an old map of the neighbourhood, its materials up close, its trade in engravings, museum objects of its kind (references/imagery.md §3). Start with `node /Users/huyin/Swingboat/english-to-be-continued/.agents/skills/taste-skill/scripts/assets.mjs "education"`, then queries from your inventory (its street, its materials, its trade's tools and history). Download what you use into the project with `--get` and credit every one on the page (`--credits` prints the footer line). They show the world around the client, never the client's own room, dish, product, person or team. Second, for what no honest picture exists of: draw the subject's own things in code, large, in one flat graphic language and the palette's real colours (its tiles, its windows, its objects; references/imagery.md §8). Third: the dealt photographs, only where an honestly generic picture fits (the sea, the weather, a material), never one a visitor could take for the thing itself and never an unrelated scene. One treatment for all of them. If this direction is built on photographs, plan the set before building (four to six pictures of one family) and reuse them at different crops and scales instead of finding or drawing a new picture for every slot; keep drawings as `<symbol>` + `<use>` or generate them with a small script so the page stays light. List every found picture (with its source and licence), every dealt photograph and every drawing that stands in for photography in your report.

Sparing. One photograph or object per chapter at most, either cut out and sitting directly on the colour, or a plain rectangle with generous colour around it. Photographs should be simple enough not to fight the field.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

## 6. Hero — Tabbed folder stack
A dark flat ground. A slim top row at 24px holds the wordmark dead centre and one text link far right at 15px. The headline is one centred line of heavy condensed capitals at clamp(96px, 10vw, 160px), spanning about 70% of the width, its cap line 110px from the top. Under it one supporting sentence in a serif at 20px, centred, max 46ch. From 51% of the height down the screen is a stack of five file folders: flat colour bands 86% of the viewport wide, centred, each offset 64px below the one behind so only a strip of the back four shows. Every folder has one trapezoid tab rising 40px from its top edge, cut with `clip-path: polygon()`, at its own horizontal position and in its own colour, holding one section name at 18px. The front folder is open and runs off the bottom edge; it carries the single action as a solid button at its top-left with 32px padding. Each folder links to its section and lends that section its ground colour. The tab shape returns as the button's top corners.

On a phone: The headline sits 96px down at 17vw on one line, or two at 15vw, centred, with the supporting sentence at 16px beneath. The folder stack starts at 48svh at full width: bands offset 52px, tabs 32px tall with names at 15px, staggered left, centre and right. The front folder runs off the bottom with the action button at full width inside it.
Headline: The name or a two-word statement on one line of up to about 12 characters at 10vw; two lines allowed on a phone.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the brand faces.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. intro — Title, note, facts row (split)
First row: a heading of two to four words in a light weight at clamp(44px, 5vw, 76px) sits on the left margin in columns 1–6; a three-line paragraph at 16px, 260px wide, sits in columns 10–12 with its last line on the heading's baseline. Columns 7–9 stay empty. 120px lower, a second row sets three or four facts (a date, a time, a place, a price) as large light text at clamp(28px, 3vw, 44px), spread across the 12 columns with `justify-content: space-between`. Each fact has a one-line explanation at 14px directly under it in the soft ink, left-aligned to the fact. Facts are separated only by the space between them, so the row reads like the line of times on a printed programme. A photograph may start under the right half of the facts row, 5 columns wide at 4:3. Padding 140px top, 120px bottom.
On a phone: Heading at 11vw on the left margin; the note follows directly under it at 16px, full width. The facts become a two-column grid with a 32px row gap, each fact at 26px with its explanation beneath. Any photograph goes last, full width.

### 2. showcase — Square plate, spare text (split)
One flat field split 50/50, at least 88svh tall. Left half: a single square photograph filling the half's width minus 40px margins, vertically centred, square corners. A large simple shape may be laid over it to turn the photograph into the object it stands for: a ring 86% of its width with a 6px stroke and a small solid dot at the centre makes a record; a notch cut by clip-path makes a ticket stub. Right half, a flex column with space-between: at the top a row of three plain text tabs at 18px (editions, years, variants), the active one in full ink and the others at 40%; in the middle the title, two to four words at 32–40px bold, with a 34ch description 48px below; along the bottom two filled rectangular cells side by side, each half the column wide and 72px tall, acting as the two actions, one in ink and one a tone off the field.
On a phone: The square photograph comes first at full width with 16px margins. The tabs sit under it as one row at 17px. Then the title at 28px, the description, and the two action cells stacked, each full width and 60px tall.

Adjusted for this page:
- It fades text: anything a visitor reads, inactive items of a list included, is `--ink` or `--muted` at full strength (contrast 4.5:1 or more). The active item is told apart by `--ink` against `--muted`, weight or the accent, not by fading the rest.

### 3. list — The printed menu sheet (band)
The list is one tall sheet of paper. A filled paper-colour panel 640px wide sits centred, or offset to columns 7–12, on a contrasting flat ground or a full-bleed photograph, with square corners and its top and bottom edges cut into shallow scallops by `clip-path` or an SVG `mask`. Inside, 64px padding and a centred, symmetrical setting like a printed card: the venue or section name at 40–48px, a one-line note at 15px, then three or four groups 56px apart. Each group opens with its name at 22px, centred. Items follow at 17px with 16px between them: the dish or service at the left of a 420px measure, the price at the right with tabular figures, and a one-line description at 14px in the soft ink under the name. Alignment alone links name and price; there are no leaders. The sheet ends with the opening hours as two centred lines. Holds 12–24 items.
On a phone: The sheet spans the screen with 10px of ground showing at each side and 28px padding; the scalloped edges stay at top and bottom. The measure is the full inner width and prices stay right-aligned. Group names at 20px, items at 16px.

### 4. about — Side facts, wide paragraph (table)
Two rows share one vertical line at 34% of the viewport width. Row one: in a 160px column at the left margin, three label and value pairs stack with 20px between them, each a label in the soft ink with its value beneath in full ink, all at 13px (founded, based in, team size). From the 34% line a five-line paragraph at clamp(20px, 1.7vw, 26px), leading 1.35, runs to 90% of the width. Row two starts 120px lower and repeats the logic for the record of the practice: one plain word at 13px at the far left (awards, press, clients), then from the 34% line two list columns, each 260px wide with a 64px gutter, at 13px with 10px between lines: name at the left, year at the right in the soft ink. No heading; the paragraph opens the section. Each list comfortably holds 6–12 lines. No photographs are needed.
On a phone: Everything aligns to the left margin. The paragraph comes first at 19px. The three label and value pairs follow as a two-column grid at 14px. The two record lists stack with 40px between them, each opened by its plain word in full ink.

### 5. proof — Plain names in columns (list)
A typographic list with no logos. The heading, two or three words at 28–32px, sits in columns 1–3 and stays top-aligned with the first row of names. Columns 5–12 hold the names in three or four text columns (`columns: 4; column-gap: 32px`), each name on its own line at 20–22px with leading 1.5, sentence case, in the full ink. Where names fall into kinds (clients, press, partners), each kind starts a new column and its first line names the kind in the softer ink at the same size, so no small labels are needed. Columns are of unequal length and are not balanced; ragged bottoms are intended. Twelve to thirty names work best. There are no boxes, bullets or separators: the column edges and the shared baseline do the organising. Leave 120px above and below.
On a phone: The heading leads at 26px. Names follow in two columns at 17px with a 20px gap; each kind begins after a 32px break with its name in the softer ink on the first line. Long names wrap inside their column rather than shrinking.

### 6. faq — Accordion of filled tiles (stack)
A two-part row. Columns 1–4 hold the heading, two to four words at about 4vw, `position: sticky; top: 96px` so it stays while the list scrolls past. Columns 5–12 hold the accordion: each question is a filled tile one step from the page ground, with 8px between tiles, no border and the page's one radius (or its motif's corner). A closed tile is 72px tall with the question at 20px medium on the left, 24px padding, and on the right a plus sign drawn from two 3px bars 16px long. The open tile switches to the accent colour with its assigned ink, the plus becomes a minus by rotating one bar, and the answer appears at 17px, no wider than 52ch. Build each tile as `<details>` with a shared `name` so one opens at a time. Six to nine tiles.
On a phone: The heading sits above at 9vw, not sticky. Tiles run full width with 6px gaps, questions at 18px wrapping to two lines, closed height at least 64px. The open tile keeps the accent colour, and answers are set at 16px with 20px padding.

### 7. footer — Contact as the headline (statement)
A full-bleed field in one saturated palette colour, at least 90svh tall. Top row, 40px from the edge: two or three short notes at 15px in columns 1–7 (address, hours, a one-line description) and a back-to-top text link at the right margin. Then a tall empty gap. In the lower half, the way to get in touch is the display line: the telephone number or the email address itself, set at 8–9vw, flush left, hard against the margin, as one link. An email address may break once, before the @, giving two lines. Size it with `clamp()` so the longest line fills at least 80% of the width. Under it, one row of small text at 14px spread across three positions: links at the left, credits in the centre, city and year at the right. The wordmark appears only small, in the top row.
On a phone: Notes stack at the top at 15px. The contact line sits in the lower half at 12vw; a telephone number stays on one line, an email breaks before the @ and is sized so each half fits the width. The meta row becomes three stacked lines at 13px.

## 8. Motion — Masked headline
On load, each line of the headline rises from behind its own baseline, about 900ms with a strong ease-out, the second line 110ms after the first; the supporting sentence and the action then fade up together. Nothing else on the page performs. Because headlines are two lines at most, you author the line breaks and wrap each line yourself. Recipe: 'Masked line reveal' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Colour at scale. A whole screen of one saturated colour is rich on its own, so a chapter needs few elements; it still needs its content at full size, because a coloured screen with one small line on it reads as empty.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Check contrast on every field with the palette's assigned ink. Do not put cards on the fields. Do not use more than six colours. Two neighbouring fields should differ clearly in lightness as well as hue.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
