The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
Every section is a chapter with its own flat colour, floor to ceiling. The page reads like a set of posters hung in a row, held together by one typeface and one grid. The colours are not decoration: they come from the subject (the brand's packaging, the artist's record sleeves, the product's own colour ways, the categories of the content) and each chapter's colour means that chapter. Decide the sequence like a designer orders a book: a strong opener, a quiet second, a surprise in the middle, a dark or very light closer.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
Sections are full-bleed fields, each at least 90svh tall, butted directly against each other with no gap and no divider. Inside every field the same 12-column grid and the same margins apply, and each field holds one thing: a huge heading (8–12vw) with a small paragraph, or one photograph with a caption, or one list. Text colour is chosen per field for strong contrast (ink or paper, never grey). Headings start on the same left edge in every chapter, so the eye feels the system as colours change. The navigation and the footer take the colours of the first and last chapter.

## 3. Type
Kept from the brand. One typeface in two weights. Headings very large and tight; body 18–20px. Because colour is loud, the type stays plain: no italics, no second face.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. Four to six flat fields used at full strength, one per section, each with its assigned text colour. No tints, no gradients between them: the hard edge where two fields meet is the look.

## 5. Imagery

Start with `node /Users/huyin/Swingboat/english-to-be-continued/.agents/skills/taste-skill/scripts/assets.mjs "education"`, then queries from your inventory (its street, its materials, its trade's tools and history). Download what you use into the project with `--get` and credit every one on the page (`--credits` prints the footer line). They show the world around the client, never the client's own room, dish, product, person or team. Second, for what no honest picture exists of: draw the subject's own things in code, large, in one flat graphic language and the palette's real colours (its tiles, its windows, its objects; references/imagery.md §8). Third: the dealt photographs, only where an honestly generic picture fits (the sea, the weather, a material), never one a visitor could take for the thing itself and never an unrelated scene. One treatment for all of them. If this direction is built on photographs, plan the set before building (four to six pictures of one family) and reuse them at different crops and scales instead of finding or drawing a new picture for every slot; keep drawings as `<symbol>` + `<use>` or generate them with a small script so the page stays light. List every found picture (with its source and licence), every dealt photograph and every drawing that stands in for photography in your report.

Sparing. One photograph or object per chapter at most, either cut out and sitting directly on the colour, or a plain rectangle with generous colour around it. Photographs should be simple enough not to fight the field.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

## 6. Hero — Stacked name, two inks
One saturated flat field covers the whole 1440×900 viewport and there is no image. The name is broken into two lines and set flush left from a 40px margin at clamp(110px, 15vw, 230px), heavy weight, tracking -0.04em, line-height 0.82 so the lines almost touch. The block starts 14% down and fills the top 58% of the screen; the longer line reaches at least column 11. Line one is set in the light ink and line two in the dark ink, so the break reads as a change of voice: split a two-word name between the lines, or break one long name with a hyphen at a syllable. The top edge carries only small text at 15px, 24px in: a monogram top-left, and two or three text links plus the single action as an underlined link top-right. The supporting sentence, 18px in the light ink and max 30ch, is right-aligned in the bottom-right corner on columns 9–12, its last line 40px above the bottom edge. The bottom-left corner stays empty. The next section begins as a different flat field exactly at the fold.

On a phone: The field fills 100svh. The name keeps its two lines and two inks at 21vw, flush left at a 16px margin, starting 18% down; a long name hyphenates rather than dropping below 18vw. The supporting sentence moves to the bottom-left at 16px across the full width, with the action as an underlined link beneath it, 24px above the bottom edge. The top-right links reduce to one word.
Headline: The name on exactly two lines, one per ink, each line up to about 8 characters at 15vw on desktop and 21vw on a phone.

Adjusted for this page:
- Its action is a text link: the primary action on the first screen is a real button (filled or solid-bordered, 44px or taller), placed where the blueprint puts the link. A second action may stay a link.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. showcase — Two objects, two fields (split)
Two panels side by side, each 50vw wide and 92svh tall, in two different flat tones from the palette, meeting on a clean vertical edge with no gap. Each panel is dominated by one cut-out object photograph scaled to 130–150% of the panel's width and positioned so the panel's top edge crops it: the reader sees the lower two-thirds of the object, very close. On light panels use mix-blend-mode: multiply to lose a white studio background. Along the bottom of each panel, 28px in from the edges, one row: the name at 20px medium and a price or fact at 17px on the left; on the right, two small rectangular actions, one filled with ink and one with paper. Nothing sits in the middle of a panel. The heading, two to four words at 32–40px, is set at the top-left of the first panel only, in the empty corner beside the object.
On a phone: Panels stack, each 78svh tall, the object scaled to 150% of the width and cut by the top and right edges. The bottom row becomes two lines: name and fact, then the two actions side by side at full width. The heading sits top-left on the first panel at 26px.

### 2. work — Stacked folders with tabs (band)
Projects are filed as folders: four to six full-width flat colour bands stacked so that each shows a 64px strip above the next, built with `position: sticky` and increasing `top` offsets (0, 64, 128px) so the bands pile up as the page scrolls. From the top-left edge of each band one or two tabs rise: filled shapes 44px tall and 200–320px wide with shouldered corners cut by `clip-path: polygon()` or an inline SVG path, side by side with 6px gaps, each in a flat colour that contrasts with its band. The project name sits inside the tab at 26–28px; the category sits right-aligned in the band's visible strip at 15px. An open band is 80svh: a 16:10 photograph in columns 1–7 and, top-aligned in columns 9–12, a 60-word description with a link. A two-word heading at 32px precedes the stack.
On a phone: Bands do not stick; they stack in normal flow, each led by one tab 40px tall with the name at 20px and the category beside it. Inside a band the photograph is full width at 4:3 with the description beneath at 16px and 20px padding.

### 3. about — Two columns, highlighted words (statement)
The story is set as text far larger than anything else on the page: 110–160 words at clamp(22px, 2.1vw, 32px), tight leading 1.18, in the soft ink, flowing through two columns that fill the full width between 24px margins (`columns: 2; column-gap: 32px`, justified, with hyphenation on). No heading. The name at the start is in full ink. Six or seven single words or short phrases carry a flat highlighter background in three or four palette colours, built with an inline `background-color`, `box-decoration-break: clone` and 0.1em of horizontal padding; they mark the facts a scanning reader wants (a place, a year, a client, a skill). The final short sentence is highlighted entire in the strongest colour and is the link to contact. Padding 120px top and bottom. No image and no portrait.
On a phone: One column at 20px, left-aligned and unjustified, leading 1.25, with 18px margins. Keep four or five highlights so no line carries two. The final highlighted sentence sits on its own line with 24px above it and works as a full-width tap target.

### 4. list — Cards that fold into spines (pinned)
For a journal, a run of notes or a list of chapters without pictures. A wrapper about 400svh tall holds a sticky stage of 100svh. The heading, two to four words at clamp(48px, 4.2vw, 64px), sits top-left on the 32px margin, 120px from the top. Under it a band 360px tall spans the content width and holds a horizontal deck of text cards, each about 620px wide, in three neighbouring flat tones of one hue taken in turn. On a card, with 32px padding: its title at 30px on up to two lines, leading 1.1; an excerpt of two lines at 16px; and along the bottom edge the date on the left and a text link to read it on the right, both at 16px. As the page scrolls, each step slides the next card in from the right edge and squeezes the earlier ones into slivers 120px wide stacked edge to edge at the left, like book spines, where only the first letters of their titles show, clipped by the sliver. Clicking a sliver brings its card back. 48px under the band, the number of entries and a link to the full list sit at 17px on the left margin, so the stage does not end on an empty floor.
On a phone: No pin and no slivers. The cards stack at full width with 12px gaps, each in its tone, the title at 24px, the excerpt at 16px, the date and link at the foot. The heading sits above them at 10vw.

Adjusted for this page:
- It pins something for three screens or more: hold a pin for two or three screens at most, give every screen under it its own content, and let nothing pass over the pinned words (the inspector checks all three).

## 8. Motion — Masked headline
On load, each line of the headline rises from behind its own baseline, about 900ms with a strong ease-out, the second line 110ms after the first; the supporting sentence and the action then fade up together. Nothing else on the page performs. Because headlines are two lines at most, you author the line breaks and wrap each line yourself. Recipe: 'Masked line reveal' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Colour at scale. A whole screen of one saturated colour is rich on its own, so a chapter needs few elements; it still needs its content at full size, because a coloured screen with one small line on it reads as empty.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Check contrast on every field with the palette's assigned ink. Do not put cards on the fields. Do not use more than six colours. Two neighbouring fields should differ clearly in lightness as well as hue.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
